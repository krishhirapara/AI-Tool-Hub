Best Free AI Tools for Students in India (2026 Guide)

By Krish Hirapara · Published November 2026 · Last checked: November 2026

My experience

ChatGPT is the tool I use most, and I have used it since 2023. For my exams, I give it an image of my syllabus and my question paper format, and ask it to generate questions and answers in that format. I also use ChatGPT to write prompts for image and video generation.

For my resume and interview preparation, I use ChatGPT and Perplexity. They helped me find good projects for my resume and helped me build those projects.

I have also tried image generation in ChatGPT and Gemini. Both of them sometimes give a wrong image, so I always check the result before I use it.

Why students should use AI tools

AI tools can save time and make learning simple. Students can use them to:

  • Understand difficult topics in simple words.
  • Make notes and summaries.
  • Create flashcards and quizzes.
  • Practise questions for exams.
  • Improve writing and grammar.
  • Make presentations and projects.
  • Learn coding and solve maths problems.
  • Do research for assignments.

AI should be used as a study helper, not as a way to copy answers. Always check the information and understand the topic yourself.

More about the best free AI tools for students

1. ChatGPT

ChatGPT is one of the most popular AI tools for students. You can ask it questions and get simple explanations.

You can use ChatGPT to:

  • Explain difficult topics.
  • Make study notes.
  • Create practice questions.
  • Improve your writing.
  • Get ideas for projects.
  • Learn coding.
  • Prepare for interviews.

For example, you can ask: “Explain photosynthesis in simple words” or “Give me 10 practice questions on algebra.”

2. Google Gemini

Google Gemini is another useful AI tool for students. It can help with study questions, writing, research and creative work.

Students can use Gemini to:

  • Get answers to study doubts.
  • Make summaries of long topics.
  • Create project ideas.
  • Write emails and drafts.
  • Practise English.
  • Make study plans.

Gemini also works well with Google tools like Gmail, Google Docs and Google Drive.

3. NotebookLM

NotebookLM is a great AI tool for studying from your own notes. You can upload PDFs, notes or study material and ask questions about them.

It can help you:

  • Summarise long chapters.
  • Make questions from your notes.
  • Create study guides.
  • Find important points.
  • Revise before exams.

This tool is useful because it gives answers based on the material you upload, so you can study from your own syllabus and notes.

4. Perplexity

Perplexity is useful for research. It gives answers with sources, so you can check where the information came from.

Students can use it for:

  • School and college research.
  • Current affairs.
  • Project work.
  • Assignment research.
  • Finding reliable information.

When you use Perplexity, always read the sources and do not copy information directly.

5. Canva

Canva is a design tool that students can use for presentations, posters, charts and projects.

You can use Canva to:

  • Make presentations.
  • Create posters and infographics.
  • Design project reports.
  • Make charts and diagrams.
  • Create resumes and social media posts.

Canva has many ready-made templates, so you can make good-looking projects without advanced design skills.

6. Grammarly

Grammarly helps students improve their English writing. It checks grammar, spelling and sentence clarity.

You can use it for:

  • Assignments.
  • Emails.
  • Applications.
  • Reports.
  • Resume writing.
  • Project documentation.

It is helpful for students who want to write clearly and avoid common grammar mistakes.

7. Photomath

Photomath is useful for maths students. You can scan a maths problem and get a step-by-step solution.

It can help with:

  • Algebra.
  • Geometry.
  • Trigonometry.
  • Calculus.
  • Basic maths problems.

Use it to understand the steps, not just to copy the answer.

8. Duolingo

Duolingo is an app for learning languages. It is useful for students who want to improve their English or learn a new language. It uses short lessons, games and daily practice to make learning fun.

How to use AI tools properly

AI can be very helpful, but students should use it in the right way.

  • Use AI to understand topics, not only to get answers.
  • Check facts before using them in assignments.
  • Do not copy AI content directly.
  • Use AI to make practice questions and revise.
  • Learn coding by writing code yourself.
  • Use AI feedback to improve your writing.
  • Do not share personal details with AI tools.

Best for assignments and research

For explaining difficult topics, ChatGPT, Claude and Google Gemini are the three general chatbots to try. They all work by chat and have a free plan with limits, so the best way to choose is to test each one on a real topic from your syllabus. ChatGPT is a flexible all-rounder. Claude is often liked for clear writing and for working with long text. Gemini is handy if you already live inside a Google account.

When your assignment needs sources, Perplexity is a better starting point because it lists the web pages behind its answers. Open those pages, read them, and cite the originals rather than the AI summary. Whatever you use, follow your college's rules on AI. Use these tools to understand and to check your work, then write the final answer in your own words.

Our pick: Perplexity, because it lists the sources behind its answers.

Best for notes and exam revision

NotebookLM is built around your own material. You add your notes or readings, then ask questions or request summaries based on them. That makes it useful in the weeks before exams, because the answers come from what you gave it. Pair it with a chatbot: ask for the key points from one unit, then ask to be quizzed on them. Always compare summaries with your original notes, because any AI tool can miss or misread something.

Our pick: NotebookLM, because its answers come from your own notes.

Best for presentations and design

Gamma can build a first draft of a slide deck from a short prompt or outline. Canva is a drag-and-drop design tool with many templates for posters, slides and graphics, and it also has some AI features. A simple workflow: write your own outline, let Gamma create the first draft, then fix the facts and add your own examples. If you prefer to control the design yourself, start from a Canva template. Both have free plans, but some features may need a paid plan.

Our pick: Gamma, for a fast first draft.

Best for coding

GitHub Copilot suggests code inside editors such as VS Code and can explain code you do not understand. It is a good learning partner if you use it carefully: write the function yourself first, then compare it with the suggestion. Students may be able to apply for access through GitHub's education programme, so check GitHub's official page. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can also explain errors and walk through code step by step.

Our pick: GitHub Copilot, for suggestions inside your editor.

Best for resumes and careers

Your resume and internship emails are where clean writing matters most. Grammarly checks grammar, spelling and clarity, and it is helpful if English is not your first language. Canva has resume templates; keep yours to one clear page and export it as a PDF. You can also ask ChatGPT or Claude to help you practise interview questions, then answer them out loud in your own words. Never invent experience or skills on a resume, whatever a tool suggests.

Our pick: Grammarly, for grammar and clarity.

Something different: audio

ElevenLabs turns text into natural-sounding speech. Students can use it for voiceovers in project videos, or to listen to a summary of their notes while travelling. Never copy someone's voice without their permission, and follow the platform's rules.

How to choose the right tool

Start with one problem, not ten tools. Pick the task that costs you the most time this week and try one free tool on it. Test with real work, such as a topic from your syllabus or a draft you already wrote, because that tells you more than any demo. Finally, check the limits. Free plans often have usage limits that can change, so look at the official site before you rely on a tool for a deadline or pay for anything.

Use AI honestly

AI tools make mistakes, and they can sound very sure when they are wrong. Treat their output as a helpful draft, not a final answer. Check important facts, numbers and names in your textbooks or trusted sources, and be open about your AI use if your college asks.
