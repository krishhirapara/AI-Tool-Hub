How to Write Good AI Prompts: A Beginner's Guide with Examples

By Krish Hirapara · Published November 2026 · Last checked: November 2026

My experience

I use ChatGPT to help me write prompts. Most of the prompts I need are for image generation and video generation. First, I tell ChatGPT what type of prompt I need and what I want to create. Then it writes the prompt for me.

These prompts work well for me. Most of the time I get the result I wanted. When a result is not right, I go back and add more details about what I want. I have learned that the clearer I am about my goal, the better the outcome.

What is an AI prompt?

A prompt is the instruction or question you give to an AI tool. If your prompt is unclear, AI may give a general or wrong answer. If your prompt is clear, AI can give a better and more useful answer. For example, “Explain photosynthesis.” is a simple prompt. AI will give you an answer, but it may not match your class level or exam needs. A better prompt is:

“Explain photosynthesis in simple words for a Class 10 student. Give important points and one easy example.”

This prompt gives AI more details, so the answer will be more useful.

Why good prompts are important

Good prompts help you get better answers. They save time and reduce confusion. A good prompt can help AI understand:

  • What you want.
  • Your class or level.
  • The subject and topic.
  • The format you need.
  • How long the answer should be.
  • Whether you need examples or practice questions.

Clear and specific prompts give better results. Adding context, task details and format makes AI answers more useful.

Simple formula for a good prompt

You can use this easy formula: Role + Task + Details + Format

1. Role

Tell AI who it should act like. Example: “Act as a science teacher.”

2. Task

Tell AI what you want it to do. Example: “Explain the water cycle.”

3. Details

Add your class, subject and level. Example: “I am a Class 8 student.”

4. Format

Tell AI how you want the answer. Example: “Give the answer in short bullet points with one example.”

Examples of good prompts

For understanding a topic

Weak prompt: “Explain gravity.”

Better prompt: “Explain gravity in simple words for a Class 9 student. Give two real-life examples and keep the answer short.”

For making notes

Weak prompt: “Make notes on the French Revolution.”

Better prompt: “Make short revision notes on the French Revolution for a Class 9 history student. Include important dates, causes and results in bullet points.”

For practice questions

Weak prompt: “Give me questions.”

Better prompt: “Give me 10 multiple-choice questions on algebra for Class 10. Include the correct answers and short explanations.”

For maths help

Weak prompt: “Solve this maths problem.”

Better prompt: “Solve this maths problem step by step. Explain each step in simple words and tell me why each step is important.”

For coding help

Weak prompt: “Write code for a website.”

Better prompt: “I am a beginner in HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Help me create a simple personal portfolio website. Give the code step by step and explain each part.”

For assignment help

Weak prompt: “Write my assignment.”

Better prompt: “Help me make an outline for my assignment on climate change. I am in Class 11. Give main points, subpoints and a simple conclusion. Do not write the full assignment.”

Tips to write better prompts

1. Be clear

Do not write confusing questions. Tell AI exactly what you want. Instead of “Help me with science.” write “Explain the human digestive system in simple words for Class 7.”

2. Give context

Tell AI about your class, subject and exam. Example: “I am preparing for my Class 12 board exam. Explain this topic according to the CBSE syllabus.”

3. Ask for a format

Tell AI if you want bullet points, a table, a paragraph or a step-by-step answer. Example: “Give the answer in a table with three columns: topic, meaning and example.”

4. Ask for examples

Examples make difficult topics easier to understand. Example: “Explain this topic with one real-life example.”

5. Ask for short answers

If you need quick revision, ask AI to keep the answer short. Example: “Explain this topic in less than 150 words.”

6. Ask AI to check your work

You can ask AI to check your answers, grammar or code. Example: “Check my English paragraph for grammar mistakes and suggest improvements.”

7. Ask follow-up questions

If you do not understand the first answer, ask again. For example: “Explain it in even simpler words.” or “Give me another example.”

Common prompt mistakes to avoid

  • Writing very short prompts like “explain this.”
  • Not mentioning your class or level.
  • Asking AI to write your full assignment.
  • Not checking the answer.
  • Using difficult words in your prompt.
  • Not asking for examples or a format.
  • Trusting AI answers without checking facts.

If an AI tool gives you a vague or boring answer, the problem is usually the question, not the tool. A good prompt is simply a clear request. You do not need special tricks or secret words. This guide gives you a simple method and examples you can adapt for study, coding and career tasks.

The four-part prompt

Most good prompts have four parts. You do not always need all four, but the more you include, the better the answer tends to be.

  1. Role or context: who you are and what the situation is.
  2. Task: exactly what you want done.
  3. Details: length, level, format, points to include or avoid.
  4. Output style: bullets, table, short paragraphs, code only.

Give real context, but not private details

The tool cannot see your syllabus, your teacher or your goals unless you say so. Mention your year, subject, and what the answer will be used for. At the same time, avoid putting private information into any AI tool: Aadhaar or PAN numbers, passwords, bank details, phone numbers, or other people’s personal data. Treat a chat window like a public place.

Treat it as a conversation

The first answer is a draft. Follow up with short instructions:

  • “Make it shorter.”
  • “Explain the second point with an example.”
  • “Now rewrite it in a more formal tone.”
  • “What might be wrong or missing in your answer?”

The last one is especially useful. Asking the tool to point out weak spots in its own answer often reveals things worth checking yourself.

Show an example of what you want

If you want a certain style, paste a short sample and say “write in this style.” For data or code, show one example input and the output you expect. Examples remove a lot of guesswork.

Different tools, same method

The same method works in ChatGPT, Claude, Google Gemini and Perplexity. Perplexity is better when you want an answer with sources, so phrase your question as something you can check. For images in Ideogram or Adobe Firefly, describe the subject, style, colours and where the image will be used.

Common prompt mistakes

  • Asking several unrelated things in one message.
  • Leaving out the level, so the answer is too advanced or too basic.
  • Accepting the first answer without checking it.
  • Asking for a finished assignment instead of help with a part of it.

FAQ

What is a prompt?

A prompt is the message or instruction you give to an AI tool. A clear prompt with context and details usually gets a more useful answer.

Do I need special words to write good prompts?

No. Clear, specific plain language works well. State what you want, who it is for, and the format you need.

Why does AI give different answers to the same prompt?

These tools generate text with some variation, so answers can differ each time. If the answer matters, check it against a reliable source.

Is it safe to put personal information in a prompt?

It is best not to. Avoid sharing ID numbers, passwords, bank details or other people's private data. Check each tool's privacy policy.

Final thoughts

Be specific, give context, set a format, and then improve the answer with follow-up messages. Practice on a real task today, such as one topic from your syllabus. For tools to practise with, see our directory.
